Where it stands

Seven years,still waiting.

Cadet Sanopao has been in continuous detention since 18 September 2019, more than seven years. This page is current as of 28 October 2026.

The sentence

Convicted anyway, and sentenced as a killer

Despite its own admissions, the court found Cadet Sanopao guilty of Article of War 97 (that he "instigated or ordered" the maltreatment) and declared the conduct "tantamount to homicide by acts of torture." It imposed Reclusion Temporal, 14 years 8 months 1 day to 20 years, plus dismissal from the service: the same homicide-grade penalty imposed on his two co-accused.

He was sentenced as if for homicide aggravated by torture, though the charge sheet alleged neither, and though the court itself held that torture had never been pleaded against him.

GCM No. 2 Joint Decision, pp. 90–93

2019 to today

The long wait.

Every date below is drawn from the record or from the official papers in the case.

18 Sep 2019

Detained

Continuous detention begins, the day Dormitorio died.

5 Jun 2020

Cleared by the civilian prosecutor

OCP Baguio City dismisses all charges for lack of probable cause; affirmed on reconsideration.

31 Mar 2025

Convicted by GCM No. 2

Guilty under Article of War 97, "tantamount to homicide by acts of torture"; Reclusion Temporal, 14 years 8 months 1 day to 20 years, plus dismissal.

Jun 2025

Pay stopped

His pay and allowances are withheld.

1 Jun 2026

Review ordered

The Chief of Staff, AFP directs the Judge Advocate General and J1 to comprehensively review the cases of personnel held at the AFP Custodial Detention Center (Memo 243-01 Jun26).

11 Jun 2026

Appeal filed

Appeal to the Chief of Staff, AFP under Presidential Decree No. 1199, with 333 pages of annexes. As of the date of this page, it has not been decided.

What the law still requires

Two safeguards remain.

I

Approval by the Chief of Staff

The decision itself provides that the penalty "shall begin to be executed upon approval by the Chief of Staff, Armed Forces of the Philippines as Convening Authority."

Joint Decision, p. 93

II

Confirmation by the President

Because he is a cadet, any sentence extending to his dismissal requires confirmation by the President before it is carried into execution.

Article of War 47(c), as amended by R.A. 242

He does not ask anyone to forget Cadet Dormitorio.

He asks only that the record be read as it is written, and that a man not be punished as a killer for a death the evidence places in other hands, at another hour.
