The law set aside

Eleven times,the law gave way.

A conviction is not only a question of evidence; it is a question of law. Each departure below is stated with what happened, the specific authority it violated, and what that law actually requires.

  1. 01 · Punished for a crime that was never charged
  2. 02 · A military court imposed a civilian penalty it had no power to impose
  3. 03 · The acts of others were blamed on him, though conspiracy was not to be proved
  4. 04 · Convicted on an opinion the court had shut out, from a witness who was never an expert
  5. 05 · The exculpatory eyewitnesses were shut out
  6. 06 · The court filled the gap with "judicial notice" of its own cadet experience
  7. 07 · The court called its own medico-legal witnesses without the defense
  8. 08 · The presiding officer sat in judgment of his own institution
  9. 09 · Pre-trial rights were denied after an order for a "mabilisang imbestigasyon"
  10. 10 · The burden of proof was turned upside down

01

Punished for a crime that was never charged

The charge was only Article of War 97: instigating maltreatment. Yet the dispositive branded the conduct "homicide by acts of torture" and used those unpleaded acts of "torture" to push the penalty to its maximum, even after the court itself held that torture had never been alleged against him.

Violated1987 Constitution, Art. III, Sec. 14(1)–(2)R.A. 9745, Sec. 22People v. Natindim, G.R. 201867 (2020)
The law

An accused has the constitutional right to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation against him. Any fact that raises the penalty must itself be alleged in the charge. A person cannot be sentenced for an offense (or an aggravating circumstance) that was never charged and that he was never given the chance to meet.

02

A military court imposed a civilian penalty it had no power to impose

The court recharacterized the offense as homicide under Article 249 of the Revised Penal Code and imposed Reclusion Temporal, a civilian penalty. That merged a military disciplinary charge with civilian penal statutes.

ViolatedR.A. 7055, Sec. 1MCM, Secs. 7, 11, 12Gonzales v. Abaya, G.R. 164007 (2006)Navales v. Abaya, G.R. 162318 (2004)
The law

Under R.A. 7055, when a member of the AFP commits an act penalized under the Revised Penal Code, jurisdiction belongs to the civil courts, not a court-martial. A court-martial's power reaches only offenses under the Articles of War. As Gonzales v. Abaya holds, a service member may be tried under the Articles of War only independently of, and separately from, any charge for the same act penalized under the Revised Penal Code.

03

The acts of others were blamed on him, though conspiracy was not to be proved

Having confirmed it would not prove conspiracy, the prosecution could charge Cadet Sanopao only with his own act. Yet the death and the blows struck by others were laid at his door, and he drew the same penalty as his co-accused.

Prosecution, TSN 5 Feb 2021, p. 7. On 5 February 2021 the prosecution confirmed on the record its earlier admission, made at the pre-trial of 6 March 2020, that it did "not intend to prove the existence of conspiracy among CDTs Sanopao, Lumbag and Imperial."
ViolatedRevised Penal Code, Arts. 8 & 17(2)People v. Yanson-Dumancas, G.R. 133527-28 (1999)People v. Hirang, 803 Phil. 277 (2017)
The law

Without conspiracy, the maxim "the act of one is the act of all" does not apply: each accused answers only for his own act. To convict a "principal by inducement," the inducement must be the determining cause of the crime, so direct and powerful as to amount to a command. Asking a plebe to find missing boots is not such an inducement, and Cadet Imperial testified that the exercises were his own initiative.

04

Convicted on an opinion the court had shut out, from a witness who was never an expert

The only thing linking Cadet Sanopao to an "order" was the pre-trial investigating officer's personal opinion that telling a plebe to find his boots was "encouraging maltreatment," an opinion he grounded expressly in his own memories as a former cadet. Lt. Col. Kalang-ad was never qualified as an expert. When the Law Member offered to treat him as an expert or resource person, Cadet Sanopao's counsel elected on the record to treat him only as an ordinary witness, and the Law Member then barred any further questions about his personal experience as a cadet.

His cadet-experience opinion was therefore objected to and shut out at trial. Yet the convicting decision relied on that very opinion as the basis of the conviction, describing counsel as having been "amenable" to treating him as a "reliable resource person" (Joint Decision, p. 75).

ViolatedMCM, Sec. 78 (proof beyond reasonable doubt)Rules of Court, Rule 130 (opinion evidence)TSN 30 Jun 2021, pp. 24–25
The law

Only a qualified expert may give opinion testimony; an ordinary witness's opinion is not competent proof of another person's intent. Once the court itself shut out his experience-based opinions, that evidence was gone, and evidence a court has excluded cannot be revived to convict.

05

The exculpatory eyewitnesses were shut out

The affidavits of Cadet Salvador and Cadet Burong were declared irrelevant and excluded, after the prosecution dismissed the very tenor of the only alleged order as irrelevant.

On that footing the two roommates were excused. Their affidavits were formally tendered as excluded evidence at the promulgation hearing on 23 June 2023.

Violated1987 Constitution, Art. III, Sec. 14MCM, Sec. 49(F) & (G)ICCPR, Art. 14(3)(e)
The law

Due process guarantees the accused a genuine opportunity to present his defense and his witnesses. Excluding the only witnesses to the actual words of the alleged order removed the defense from the decisive issue in the case.

06

The court filled the gap with "judicial notice" of its own cadet experience

Confronted with its own admission of "no direct evidence," the panel did not acquit. At page 78 it declared that it "applied the judicial notice rule in appreciating the testimony of Lt. Col. Kalang-ad, as more than the majority of its members are themselves graduates of the Philippine Military Academy and had undergone the same ordeal."

ViolatedJoint Decision, p. 78MCM, Sec. 125Rules of Court, Rule 129, Sec. 2State Prosecutors v. Judge Muro, A.M. RTJ-92-876 (1994)
The law

Judicial notice lets a court accept, without proof, only facts that are indisputable and commonly known. A disputed fact at the heart of the case can never be "noticed," and a judge may not decide on facts he knows only personally.

07

The court called its own medico-legal witnesses without the defense

On 19 May 2023 the panel called the PNP medico-legal officer, Police Maj. Nolan Genova, as "a witness for the court," and on 2 June 2023 it did the same with the NBI's Dr. Ronald Bandonill. They testified to the two decisive facts of the case: the cause of death and when the fatal injury was inflicted. Cadet Sanopao's counsel of choice, Atty. Ma. Concepcion Santiago, was not present. Neither witness was cross-examined by the defense at all.

181questions to PMaj. Genovapanel 176 · prosecutor 5 · defense 0
76questions to Dr. Bandonillpanel 70 · prosecutor 6 · defense 0
ViolatedMCM, Secs. 45, 49 & 751987 Constitution, Art. III, Sec. 14(2)Tabuena v. Sandiganbayan, G.R. 103501-03 (1997)People v. Opida, G.R. L-46272 (1986)
The law

A witness for the court is still a witness who must be confronted, and the accused keeps his right to counsel of his choice. When the judges ask about 97% of the questions and the defense asks none, the tribunal has "descended into the arena" (Tabuena; Opida).

08

The presiding officer sat in judgment of his own institution

Brig. Gen. Paulo Teodoro sat as a member throughout the trial and presided over the court that deliberated and promulgated the decision. He was also the Assistant Superintendent of the Philippine Military Academy, the very institution whose culture and practices the panel set out to judge.

ViolatedMCM, Sec. 58(e)Dacera v. Judge Dizon, A.M. RTJ-00-1573
The law

A judge must have no personal or institutional interest in the outcome; litigants are entitled to "the cold neutrality of an impartial judge."

09

Pre-trial rights were denied after an order for a "mabilisang imbestigasyon"

The academy's first investigator, Capt. Gamos, testified that because the death had become a "national issue," the Commandant, then Brig. Gen. Bartolome Bacarro, ordered them to conduct a "mabilisang imbestigasyon" (a quick investigation) (TSN 5 Feb 2021, p. 13). On the stand, the officers who handled the case admitted what that speed cost.

ViolatedArticle of War 71MCM, Secs. 30(c) & 35(a)Cudia v. Superintendent, PMA, G.R. 211362 (2015)
The law

Article of War 71 requires a thorough and impartial investigation in which the accused is given full opportunity to cross-examine witnesses and present a defense. As the Supreme Court held in Cudia, "the PMA is not immune from the strictures of due process."

10

The burden of proof was turned upside down

At the same session of GCM No. 2 on 12 May 2023, in the separate case of another cadet charged over the same incident, a panel member who sat through Cadet Sanopao's trial, Col. Resurreccion, pressed that cadet to prove his own innocence:

The demand was addressed to another cadet, not to Cadet Sanopao. But it came from a member of the same court, at the same session, and it laid bare the standard that court was applying.

Violated1987 Constitution, Art. III, Sec. 14(2)MCM, Sec. 78ICCPR, Art. 14(2)
The law

Every accused is presumed innocent, and the burden of proving guilt beyond reasonable doubt rests on the prosecution alone. It never shifts.

11

The panel prompted the prosecutor against a defense witness

On 15 September 2023, while Lt. Col. Ramon Gualberto, a defense witness for co-accused Cadet Imperial, was testifying about the loopholes in the investigation, a panel member turned to the Trial Judge Advocate and prompted the objection:

ViolatedDacera v. Judge Dizon, A.M. RTJ-00-1573People v. Opida, G.R. L-46272 (1986)
The law

The Trial Judge Advocate is the prosecutor; the panel is the judge. A tribunal that prompts the prosecution's objections against a defense witness has stopped being a neutral adjudicator.
